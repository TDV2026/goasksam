The analyst's five questions

An analyst clearing 30 to 50 leads a day asks the same five things of every car. The screen answers them in reading order, on one page, with one place to decide.

  • Is this the car the seller says it is? Photos first and large, then eight facts, each with where it came from.
  • What is missing? Needs attention sits in the decision panel, never buried in a tab, with the action on the row.
  • What did cars like this sell for? One range, one median, one count, one period. Every sale drawn as a dot so the shape of the market is visible.
  • Which sales back my number? The closest sales with photo, price and one line on why it is close. Tick the ones that go in the report.
  • What is my number? One figure, the recommended reserve, always on screen, typed or dragged. Preview. Finalize.

Layout

  • Three zones at 1280 and wider: case queue 232px, the car and its evidence (fluid, scrolls), Reserve panel 372px (sticky, never scrolls away).
  • No tabs. Vehicle, evidence and decision are one page; the decision panel stays beside all of it.
  • 12px radius cards on a quiet grey page, 16 to 18px padding, 16px between cards. One shadow only, on the Reserve panel, because it is the one thing that floats.
  • Under 1100px the Reserve panel moves to the top and the queue becomes a drawer.

Visual hierarchy

  • Level 1: the recommended reserve (44px serif, copper). Nothing else on the screen is that size or that colour.
  • Level 2: the car's name and the market's typical range (26 to 28px serif).
  • Level 3: section titles 14px semibold; values 13.5px medium; labels 12px grey.
  • Colour carries meaning only: green is evidence from completed sales, copper is the analyst's decision, amber is something to resolve.

Components

  • Reserve panel: the figure, how many sales reached it, market line, condition and optional note, why, needs attention, report checks, Preview and Finalize.
  • Sales plot: one dot per sale, typical range band, median, draggable reserve marker, asking tick. Choosing a condition lights that rung's sales.
  • Period switch: 6, 12, 24 months. The engine recomputes; the page prints.
  • Fact row: label, editable value, source chip (Seller, VIN, Verified, Not confirmed).
  • Comparable row: photo, title, why line, price, In report tick, Exclude with a reason.
  • Photo strip: cover plus thumbnails, tick for the report, Select all, drop to add.
  • Documents: tiles for what is on file, a drop tile to add.
  • Next steps: suggested lines the analyst edits, removes or adds; printed as written.

Report rules

  • Page 1: cover photo across the top half, the car's name, one figure (the recommended reserve) and how many comparable cars sold for it or more. Three tiles: what most sold for, condition, mileage. Then the reason, in one or two sentences.
  • Page 2: every sale as a dot with the reserve marked, what supports it, what was taken into account, the seller's photographs.
  • Page 3: two, three or four comparable sales, each with the photograph from its own listing or no picture at all, never a substitute. An evidence note: period, population, prices before buyer premium, date updated.
  • Page 4: what happens next, set large and written by the analyst (we call you, send the invoice, agree the reserve, schedule the listing). Trend only when each quarter has enough sales.
  • A fact the seller stated reads "the seller reports" on every page until a document or photo verifies it. A reserve above what the sales support prints only as the seller's request, with the range beside it.
  • The condition caveat prints only if the analyst writes one. Listings for sale elsewhere never print. Titles come from the engine, never the typed input.
